About

I'm a PhD candidate in Information Science at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, co-advised by Professor Catherine Blake and Professor Halil Kilicoglu. My work sits at the intersection of natural language processing, cheminformatics, and chemosensory science. I am interested in how molecular structure, scientific text, and human perception of smell can be modeled together.

Before Illinois, I earned an M.S. in Library and Information Science from Pennsylvania Western University at Clarion, with a capstone on digitization advised by Dr. Danielle Reed at the Monell Chemical Senses Center, and a B.A. in Computer Science from The Evergreen State College with an emphasis in database design. My path from database systems, to library and information science, to olfactory research shapes how I think about scientific data; as something that has to be found, represented, and made usable before it can be modeled.

Research

My work covers three key threads. Hover or tap a card for more information and the publications behind it.

Chemosensory NLP

Olfactory & Chemosensory Information Extraction

Mining scientific text for what it says about smell.

Extracting structured knowledge about smell disorders, preferences, and perceptual language from unstructured scientific and consumer text, towards a unified NLP framework for chemosensory knowledge discovery.

  • Scientific Olfactory Information Extraction — MSLD 2026
  • Smell Disorders as Indicators of Neurodegenerative Disease — AChemS 2025
  • Cross-Country Comparison of Sweet Preferences — AChemS 2023
Molecular ML

Multi-Modal Models for Odor Perception

Molecule + language representations, predicting how things smell.

Integrating molecular representations from LLMs and generative AI into ensemble machine-learning models that predict odor quality and intensity; including how bifunctional molecules change character across concentration.

  • DREAM Olfaction Prediction Challenge — 1st leaderboard, 4th final round
Biomedical NLP

Information Extraction & Research Trustworthiness

Pipelines for biomedical relation extraction and scientific rigor.

Building deep-learning pipelines for biomedical relation extraction and evaluating the trustworthiness of the biomedical literature itself, from knowledge-resource reviews to scoping protocols for assessing scientific rigor.

  • Deep Learning Architectures for Biomedical Relation Extraction — Database 2024
  • Knowledge Resources for Complementary & Integrative Health — J. Nutrition 2026
  • Automated Assessment of Trustworthiness of Biomedical Publications — WCRI 2026
